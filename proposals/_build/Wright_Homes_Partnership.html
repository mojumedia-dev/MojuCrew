The story this data tells:

  1. Top of funnel is shrinking, but quality is rising. Site users dropped 46% in four months while engagement time went up 27%. Fewer visitors are coming, but the ones who arrive are more interested. The structural problem is paid-channel decay: cross-network and paid social collapsed; organic stayed flat. Reallocation of media spend is the immediate priority.

  2. Lead capture mechanics are working better, but unevenly. Contact form submissions grew 28x from January to April. Either the form was redesigned, measurement was fixed, or content drove a real conversion lift. That's a major bright spot. But chat is being used by only 0.1% of visitors (11 sessions on 10,241 visitors in April). Phone clicks are similarly flat at 49/month. Most buyer engagement is being absorbed by Agent Page Viewed (3,182 in April), meaning buyers route to individual agents, not into a brand-owned funnel where Wright captures and nurtures the relationship.

  3. The Custom Home page is broken or has been demoted. Page views dropped 94% (2,928 to 162) between January and February and never recovered. This needs immediate investigation. For a builder with custom-home capability, that's a structural funnel leak.

  4. JT Single Family is the only community trending up (3,592 to 3,886). Whatever's working there should be modeled and applied to the others.

  5. Buyers are price-sensitive. Offers Page views grew 77% while community pages contracted. The market is asking for value; the funnel should answer with smart promotion content and dynamic offer messaging.

The realistic conversation with Hajime starts with reallocation, not a topline increase. Real waste exists in the current $500K spend. AI's first job is to find and reinvest it.

What reallocation looks like, identified from Wright's existing media plan and Q1 GA data:

  • Billboards: $5,000/month, $60K/year with no measurable conversion attribution. AI continuous attribution would never have allocated this in the first place; reallocating it into digital channels alone funds material lift in lead volume.
  • Print spend (~$2,700/month) is likewise unattributable in a digital-first builder funnel. Strong candidate to redirect into Google Performance Max and Meta Advantage+.
  • No paid third-party listings ($0 on Zillow / Realtor.com paid placement). Wright's competitors are paying for top-of-listing position.
  • Cross-network spend collapsed 82% Jan to Apr. Needs immediate audit. Either budget was pulled mid-quarter, audiences fatigued, or the channel structurally stopped working. AI flags this in real time, not at quarterly review.
  • Paid Social down 61% while engagement time went up. Fewer visitors but more engaged. The audiences Paid Social IS reaching are the right ones; the problem is volume, not quality. Smart bid expansion can recover the volume without losing quality.

The headline argument: A continuously-optimizing AI doesn't audit waste once. It identifies inefficiency every hour, in real time, and reallocates the freed dollars into channels that convert.

That's roughly $80 to $90K per year of currently low-ROI spend that AI can redirect into proven-performance channels in the first 60 days of the partnership, before any conversation about increasing the topline budget.

Built to Layer on Lasso

Wright is adopting Lasso as the CRM and email marketing platform. MojuCrew is designed to integrate cleanly into that decision and amplify it. The integration is concrete:

  • MojuChat to Lasso: every qualified lead from the AI sales counselor pushes into Lasso instantly via REST API, with conversation transcript, qualification answers, and intent score attached. Lasso is the system of record; MojuChat is the front door.
  • AI enrichment back to Lasso: lead scores, next-best-action recommendations, predicted conversion probability, and engagement-channel preferences all written onto the Lasso contact record so the sales team works from one source of truth.
  • Lasso email marketing, AI-fueled: MojuContent drafts subject lines, body copy, and full templates at scale across every community and segment. Lasso continues to handle the send and the deliverability infrastructure. Builders get content velocity without losing the CRM workflow they're already adopting.
  • MojuReviews to Lasso: every review interaction tied back to the matching contact record, so Wright sees the full buyer journey including reputation touch-points.
  • Webhook-driven sync: Lasso's webhooks notify MojuCrew of contact updates and stage changes; MojuCrew's AI re-evaluates and adjusts campaigns and follow-ups in real time.
  • Unified Hajime dashboard: pulls from both Lasso (CRM, email engagement, contact lifecycle) and MojuCrew (AI activity, ad performance, content production) into a single executive view.

The result is one cohesive funnel. Lasso runs the rolodex and the email engine. MojuCrew runs the AI capability that makes the rolodex perform. Wright pays no separate AI software license, no integration consultancy fee, and no second CRM. The integration scope is included in the partnership.
